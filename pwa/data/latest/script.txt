◆ 배경 — Introduction
  · AA frequently coexists with autoimmune thyroid disease, vitiligo, atopic disorders, and other immune abnormalities.9,10 Antibodies against hair-follicle structures and various non-organ-specific antigens have been detected in patients with AA, and proteomic studies have suggested heterogeneity in the autoantigen repertoire.11,12 The clinical significance of circulating autoantibodies in AA nevertheless remains unclear: they may simply reflect shared susceptibility to autoimmunity, or they may mark more pronounced immune activation, greater disease burden, or a distinct treatment response.
  · Clinical studies that systematically evaluate antinuclear antibody (ANA) and extractable nuclear antigen (ENA) antibody profiles in conjunction with objective SALT severity and longitudinal treatment outcomes remain limited.11–13
  · ANA positivity and positivity for any of the 12 antigen-specific antibodies were combined into a single composite exposure to evaluate overall ANA/ENA-related autoantibody status across different specificities.

◆ 연구 방법 — Materials and Methods
  · SALT was calculated using standard scalp-area weights: vertex, 40%; occipital region, 24%; left temporal region, 18%; and right temporal region, 18%.
  · Using clinically established categories, SALT ≤20 was defined as mild disease and SALT >20 as moderate-to-severe disease; the latter comprised moderate disease (20<SALT<50) and severe disease (SALT ≥50).15,16 These SALT-based categories were used for the present analysis; broader morbidity features beyond scalp hair loss were not incorporated into the binary outcome.17 The primary binary baseline outcome was SALT >20.
  · SALT50 was defined as a reduction of at least 50% from baseline SALT and was evaluated using the measurements entered in the week-12 and week-24 fields of the case report form.18,19 The primary analyses used available outcomes.

◆ 고찰 — Discussion
  · In this retrospective cohort, definitive ANA/ENA-related study-panel positivity was associated with greater baseline scalp hair loss and lower SALT50 response at weeks 12 and 24.
  · Although T-cell-mediated attack on the hair follicle is central to AA, humoral immune abnormalities may also be present, and patients with AA can produce antibodies against hair-follicle components and other autoantigens.5,11 ANA/ENA positivity in this study may therefore represent a broader autoimmune background or degree of immune activation, rather than necessarily indicating directly pathogenic antibodies.
  · Among four exploratory antibody subtypes, anti-SmD1 showed the smallest raw P value for SALT >20 (P=0.018; q=0.072), and anti-snRNP showed a nominal association with continuous SALT (rank-biserial correlation, 0.779; P=0.022; q=0.086; Table S2).
  · Systematic reviews and randomized trials support the efficacy of oral JAK inhibitors in appropriate populations with AA.24,25 Baricitinib studies report responses over 36, 52, and 104 weeks, including later-response trajectories,26–29 and trials and extensions of ritlecitinib and deuruxolitinib provide further evidence of benefit.30–32 In our small positive subgroup, four JAK-treated patients with a week-24 measurement achieved SALT50.

◆ 고찰 — Strengths and Limitations
  · The positive group was small (n=18), and covariate completeness reduced the adjusted-model sample sizes to 100 at baseline, 86 at week 12 and 68 at week 24.
  · Of the 81 patients with week-24 outcomes, 19 did not achieve SALT50; the adjusted model included 68 complete cases with 16 nonresponders and six predictor terms.

◆ 결론 — Conclusion
  · In this single-center retrospective cohort, definitive positivity in the ANA/ENA-related study panel was associated with greater baseline disease burden and lower SALT50 response at 12 and 24 weeks after adjustment for measured clinical factors.
  · The findings support further study of antibody status as a possible prognostic marker.
  · The JAK subgroup observations remain descriptive, and no individual antibody-subtype association remained significant after FDR correction.
