[상세 내용] (초록 3개 항목 정리 — 이 논문은 전문이 아직 공개되지 않아 초록을 씁니다)

◆ 배경 — Background
  · Crohn's disease (CD) is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease that can be associated with extraintestinal manifestations.
  · Alopecia areata (AA) is an autoimmune non-scarring hair loss disorder.
  · Their coexistence is uncommon, and managing both conditions simultaneously is challenging when intestinal and cutaneous responses diverge.
  · Ustekinumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting the shared IL-12/IL-23 pathway, may offer a dual therapeutic benefit, but evidence in comorbid cases is limited.

◆ 배경 — Introduction
  · We report an 18-year-old man with a 2-year history of recurrent paroxysmal abdominal pain and mucoid hematochezia, as well as patchy non-scarring scalp alopecia.
  · Computed tomography enterography and colonoscopy showed segmental bowel wall thickening and diffuse pinpoint erosions; histopathological examination demonstrated non-caseating granulomas, supporting a diagnosis of CD.
  · Vedolizumab induction therapy with methylprednisolone bridging resulted in disease remission.
  · The patient's hair loss persisted, and he was diagnosed with AA.
  · After his AA failed to respond to topical corticosteroids, his treatment was switched from vedolizumab to ustekinumab (390 mg intravenously at week 0, followed by 90 mg subcutaneously every 8 weeks).
  · By week 24, marked scalp hair regrowth was evident, and by week 48, complete resolution of AA was achieved, while his CD remained in remission.

◆ 결론 — Conclusion
  · This case suggests that ustekinumab may be a feasible therapeutic option for patients with coexisting CD and refractory AA, particularly when a shared IL-12/IL-23-mediated inflammatory pathway is suspected.
  · Further prospective studies are required to confirm its efficacy and generalizability in this setting.
