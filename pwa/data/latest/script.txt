[한눈에 보기]
- Androgenetic alopecia (AGA) and seborrheic dermatitis (SD) are two common scalp disorders that frequently impair the quality of life and may coexist in a subset of patients across all genders.
- SD presents in 40%-50% of all patients diagnosed with AGA.
- Although these conditions are traditionally discussed separately, increasing evidence suggests that they may share overlapping pathogenic factors, including androgen-driven sebum hypersecretion, imbalance of the scalp microecology, barrier dysfunction, and inflammatory signaling.

◆ 배경 — 1. Introduction
  · Seborrheic dermatitis (SD) is a common, recurrent, chronic inflammatory skin disease that predominantly affects sebaceous gland‐rich areas, particularly the scalp, and is characterized by yellow‐red patches covered with greasy, yellowish scales or crusts and varying degrees of pruritus.
  · Androgenetic alopecia (AGA), formerly known as seborrheic alopecia, is a progressive androgen‐dependent disorder characterized by hair follicle miniaturization with a typical patterned distribution.
  · Although SD and AGA are distinct conditions, both exhibit high prevalence and incidence rates, with a predilection for young adults and older men.

◆ 그 밖의 내용 — 2. Clinical and Epidemiological Overlap Between SD and AGA
  · In a study by Jang et al. involving 1218 patients with AGA, SD was the most common concomitant disease in both sexes, with a prevalence of 51.2% in men and 45.7% in women.
  · Another study reported an SD prevalence of 42.1% among patients with AGA, with both prevalence and severity scores increasing in parallel with AGA severity.
