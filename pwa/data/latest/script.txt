◆ 배경 — Background (자동 구분)
  · Introduction: Alopecia areata (AA) is a chronic, immune-mediated non-scarring hair loss affecting approximately 2% of the population.
  · The approval of oral JAK inhibitors has established targeted systemic therapy for moderate-to-severe AA.
  · Incomplete response rates, relapse upon treatment discontinuation, and safety contraindications, however, leave a substantial proportion of patients requiring alternative options.

◆ 주요 결과 — Results (자동 구분)
  · Areas covered: This review evaluates the immunopathogenic rationale and available clinical evidence for biologic therapies in AA, including inhibitors of tumor necrosis factor (TNF), interleukin-4 receptor α subunit (IL-4 Rα), IL-17, and IL-23.
  · Relevant literature was identified through PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov (last search: 17 October 2026).
  · Expert opinion: No biologic has demonstrated efficacy in an adequately powered randomized controlled trial in AA.
  · A phase 2a randomized trial of dupilumab showed modest efficacy, most pronounced in patients with elevated baseline IgE.
  · Paradoxically, TNF, IL-17, and IL-23 inhibitors have been implicated in drug-induced AA, a hypothesis-generating observation that warrants cautious interpretation.
  · Given the higher bar set by JAK inhibitors and the risk of paradoxical disease, currently available cytokine-targeting biologics cannot be recommended for AA outside of clinical trials.
