[한눈에 보기]
- Methods: A multicenter, retrospective observational cohort study with a cross-sectional PRO assessment was performed in adults with severe AA (SALT score ≥50) treated with baricitinib at 15 Spanish hospitals from June 2022 and with at least 36 weeks of follow-up, regardless of whether they remained on treatment.
- Effectiveness was assessed using SALT score at treatment initiation and at 12, 24, 36, and 52 weeks, and the percentage of patients with SALT≤20.
- In subpopulations affected in specific areas, persistence rates exceeded 87%, at least 55% achieved SALT≤20 at week 52, and mean satisfaction score was above 320.

◆ 배경 — Introduction
  · Alopecia areata (AA) is a common, inflammatory, autoimmune disease characterized by non-scarring hair loss.1,2 The global prevalence of AA ranges from 0.57% to 3.8%.3,4 In 2021 in Spain, the incidence was 0.52 per 1000 patient-years, while the point prevalence was 0.44%.5 There is no difference between genders, and onset can occur at any age.6,7 Hair loss can be present in various patterns, including patchy, ophiasis, ophiasis inversa/sisaipho, reticular, and diffuse.8 Furthermore, alopecia totalis (AT), characterized by total scalp hair loss, and alopecia universalis (AU), being the complete loss of scalp and body hair, occur in 7–12% of AA patients.9 Additionally, specific areas such as the beard, eyebrows, eyelashes, and nails may also be affected.10 Although not a life-threatening disease, AA is associated with a substantial impairment of patient confidence, self-esteem and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and with sexual dysfunction.11–13
  · Although the exact mechanism is not yet understood, the loss of immune privilege in the anagen hair bulb is now widely recognized as a key factor in the pathogenesis of AA,14 and it involves multiple genetic and environmental factors.15,16 The disease is dynamic, with alternating periods of flares and remission and most patients may experience multiple relapses.17,18
  · Traditional therapy for AA includes topical treatment (corticosteroids, minoxidil, retinoids, anthralin or immunotherapy), systemic agents (corticosteroids, methotrexate, cyclosporine), or phototherapy/chemotherapy and laser.19 However, in recent years, new therapeutic options have emerged with good efficacy such as Janus kinase inhibitors (JAKi).15,20–22 In 2024, the European expert consensus statement recommended JAKi as the first-choice treatment for AA.23 Baricitinib is the first oral selective JAKi approved for treating adults with severe AA by the European Medicines Agency (EMA) in May 2022 and by the U.S.

◆ 연구 방법 — Materials and Methods
  · Follow-up assessments, when available, were conducted at Weeks 12, 24, 36, and 52.
  · Follow-up data were captured at predefined time points (12±6, 24±6, 36±6 and 52±10 weeks, when available).
  · Based on persistence estimates from clinical trials data,21 and assuming a 95% confidence level, 8% precision, and an expected proportion of patients completing 36 weeks of treatment of 91%, the minimum required sample size was estimated at 50 patients.

◆ 고찰 — Discussion
  · At week 52, the probability of persistence was 91.8%, slightly exceeding or falling within the range reported in the BRAVE-AA1 and BRAVE-AA2 clinical trials (85.9–89.7%).21 Furthermore, our findings align with real-world evidence from other countries: the multicenter French study by Mourtada et al (99 patients, 13 centers) reported a 77.8% retention rate after a median follow-up of 12 months, while the Japanese single-center study by Wada-Irimada et al (51 patients) found 70.6% persistence at 104 weeks.31,32 Overall, these data seem to indicate that persistence with baricitinib is generally maintained across different clinical contexts.
  · Baricitinib use was associated with high effectiveness, with 71.1% of patients achieving a SALT score ≤20 at 52 weeks, while clinical trials reported rates ranging from approximately 21.2% to 40.9%.21 Similarly, evidence from real-world studies has shown higher proportions of patients reaching SALT ≤20 under routine clinical practice, ranging from 31.3% to 68.8%, depending on AA severity and timing of assessment.24,31,32,36–41
  · High rates of anxiety, depression, and impaired HRQoL have been consistently reported, reinforcing the importance of understanding patients’ perspectives and ensuring treatment satisfaction in clinical practice.3,11,42 Previous studies have demonstrated that baricitinib improved patients’ psychological well-being and quality of life.24,43,44 Consistent with these findings, our study also explored patient perceptions of their treatment, showing that baricitinib is effective under real-world conditions and is associated with meaningful patient-reported satisfaction and perceived improvement.
  · Other authors also suggested that extended treatment duration with baricitinib leads to better responses and improved SALT scores.37 Moreover, in line with other real-world studies,32,37,38,43,45 baricitinib also improved eyebrow and eyelash regrowth.

◆ 결론 — Conclusions
  · In this selected, real-world cohort of adults with severe AA treated in routine clinical practice in Spain, baricitinib use was associated with high treatment persistence and patient-reported satisfaction, improved perceived scalp hair loss, and consistent improvement in SALT scores, including in patients with involvement of specific areas such as body hair, nails, beard hair, and eyebrows/eyelashes.
  · As this was a descriptive study without a comparator group, and some analyses were restricted to patients who remained on treatment, these findings should be interpreted as real-world outcomes rather than comparative or causal effectiveness.
