◆ 배경 — Introduction
  · Androgenetic alopecia (AGA), the most prevalent form of hair loss worldwide, affects approximately 50% of men by the age of 50 years and carries substantial psychosocial consequences including impaired self-esteem, body image disturbance, and reduced quality of life.1 Hair transplantation—encompassing follicular unit extraction (FUE) and follicular unit transplantation (FUT)—has emerged as the definitive surgical intervention for AGA, offering permanent redistribution of hair-bearing follicles from donor to recipient areas.2 Global demand for hair restoration procedures has accelerated markedly over the past decade, with FUE now constituting the predominant technique owing to its minimally invasive nature and favorable scarring profile.3
  · Finasteride (1 mg/day orally) and minoxidil (5% topical solution) represent the cornerstones of adjuvant medical therapy, prescribed to preserve non-transplanted follicles, reduce postoperative shedding, and support graft consolidation.4 However, adherence to these regimens is notoriously poor: a cross-sectional analysis demonstrated that fewer than 40% of patients maintained consistent minoxidil use beyond 6 months,5 while long-term adherence to finasteride is similarly compromised by safety concerns and inadequate patient education.6 Poor adherence to adjuvant therapy translates directly into suboptimal graft survival, accelerated progression of residual alopecia, and patient dissatisfaction—outcomes with substantial clinical and economic ramifications.4,5 Without sustained adjuvant therapy, residual androgenetic alopecia continues to progress along its natural course.7
  · A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis of 26 clinical trials concluded that mHealth interventions produced meaningful improvements in medication adherence compared with usual care, with particular efficacy observed for reminder-based and nurse-engaged platforms.10 A complementary scoping review identified nurse engagement, personalized reminders, and two-way communication as the most effective application design features.11 In clinical practice, nurse-led digital programs have demonstrated advantages over unguided applications by providing personalized feedback and timely triage of clinical concerns.12–14 In dermatological contexts, digital health interventions have shown positive effects on treatment adherence and patient-reported outcomes,15 with teledermatology platforms achieving comparable or superior adherence rates relative to conventional encounters.16,17

◆ 연구 방법 — Methods
  · The primary outcome was overall medication adherence measured by the validated 5-item Medication Adherence Report Scale (MARS-5) at 6 months after surgery.16 MARS-5 scores range from 5 to 25, with higher scores indicating greater adherence; scores >20, 15–20, and <15 denote high, medium, and low adherence, respectively.
  · Secondary outcomes included: (1) SMS (self-management score; 0–100 scale, higher = better), a composite measure assessing wound care compliance, activity adherence, and follow-up attendance, collected at baseline and months 1, 3, and 6; (2) Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI; 0–30 scale, lower = better quality of life), validated for alopecia populations;19 (3) adherence category distribution at 6 months; (4) graft survival rate (%) at 6 months, calculated as the number of surviving follicular units counted by digital trichoscopy (70x magnification) within three fixed 1-cm2 template zones of the recipient area, divided by the total graft count recorded in the operative note, and multiplied by 100%; trichoscopic assessments were performed by an experienced assessor who was unaware of group assignment, and formal intra- or inter-observer reproducibility testing was not performed (acknowledged as a limitation); (5) wound infection rate within 30 days; (6) follow-up visit completion rate; (7) patient satisfaction score (0–100); and (8) depressive symptom severity by the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9).20 Application engagement metrics (monthly login frequency and nurse message response rate) were recorded for intervention cohort patients.

  · The cohort comprised 178 male (89.0%) and 22 female (11.0%) patients, with a mean age of 30.90 ± 7.05 years.
  · Mean age was 31.04 ± 7.22 years (intervention) versus 30.75 ± 6.87 years (control; P = 0.771).

◆ 고찰 — Discussion
  · The magnitude of the primary adherence difference (MD 8.40 points on MARS-5 at 6 months) substantially exceeds what would be expected from pharmacological interventions alone, and the corresponding high-adherence rate of 68% in the intervention cohort versus 3% in controls underscores the potential of structured digital nursing support.
  · A 2025 meta-analysis by Kim et al (26 trials, n = 5,174) confirmed that mHealth interventions produce significant adherence improvements across chronic conditions.10 A complementary scoping review identified nurse engagement, personalized reminders, and bidirectional communication as the most effective design features,11 precisely the components embedded in our intervention.
  · Nurse-delivered psychoeducation via the messaging module may have attenuated distress associated with normal postoperative effluvium—a transient phenomenon that frequently precipitates unnecessary medication discontinuation.5 These findings are consistent with digital dermatology cohorts demonstrating that professional digital engagement improves patient-reported dermatological wellbeing.16,21 The DLQI has been validated as a responsive outcome measure in alopecia populations,19,23 supporting the methodological integrity of this assessment.
  · PHQ-9 scores at 6 months did not differ significantly between cohorts in the unadjusted analysis (P = 0.109), although the difference reached statistical significance after propensity-score weighting (adjusted MD −1.94, 95% CI −3.09 to −0.80; P = 0.001); the small magnitude of this difference warrants balanced interpretation.

◆ 결론 — Conclusions
  · This retrospective cohort study provides real-world evidence that a multicomponent nurse-led mHealth intervention—incorporating personalized medication reminders, bidirectional nurse communication, structured self-management education, and proactive nurse follow-up—is associated with clinically meaningful improvements in medication adherence, self-management behaviors, dermatological quality of life, and graft survival among patients undergoing hair transplantation.
  · The association was consistent across demographic and clinical subgroups.
  · Given the high global prevalence of AGA, the increasing volume of hair restoration procedures, and the established importance of postoperative medication adherence for sustainable outcomes, nurse-led multicomponent digital follow-up programs may represent a promising and practically feasible enhancement to postoperative care.
