[상세 내용] (초록 4개 항목 정리 — 이 논문은 전문이 아직 공개되지 않아 초록을 씁니다)

◆ 배경 — Purpose
  · Chemotherapy-induced alopecia (CIA) negatively affects patients' appearance and emotional well-being.
  · This network meta-analysis aimed to compare the efficacy of different preventive interventions for CIA.

◆ 연구 방법 — Methods
  · We searched PubMed, Embase, CENTRAL, and EBSCO Open Dissertations from inception to March 2025.
  · Randomized controlled trials evaluating interventions to prevent CIA in cancer patients and reporting hair preservation outcomes (as defined by individual trials, including CTCAE grade < 2, < 30 to < 50% hair loss, or WHO grade 0-2) were included.
  · Scalp hypothermia was divided into three groups based on duration post-chemotherapy: short (0-45 min), traditional (46-90 min), and prolonged (> 90 min).
  · Risk of bias was assessed using Cochrane RoB2.
  · Risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were pooled using a random-effects model.
  · Interventions were ranked with surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA), and certainty of evidence was evaluated using CINeMA.

◆ 주요 결과 — Results
  · Of 6696 articles identified, 12 (n = 736) evaluating nine interventions (three scalp hypothermia durations, scalp compression, four pharmacological agents, and usual care) were included.
  · Network meta-analysis indicated that prolonged-scalp hypothermia significantly improved hair preservation compared with usual care (RR = 24.66; 95%CI = 7.07-86.05; low certainty of evidence) and traditional scalp hypothermia (RR = 2.13; 95%CI = 1.15-3.93; very low certainty of evidence), and pharmacological agents (RRs ranging from 14.06 to 23.83; low certainty).
  · However, prolonged-scalp hypothermia tended to show better hair preservation than short duration, though not statistically significant (RR = 2.06, 95%CI = 0.78-5.39).
  · Based on SUCRA, prolonged-scalp hypothermia (97.0%) had the highest ranking, followed by short scalp hypothermia (78.6%) and traditional scalp hypothermia (76.8%).
  · Most studies were rated as having high risk of bias, and the certainty of evidence was graded as low to very low.

◆ 결론 — Conclusion
  · Current low certainty evidence suggests that scalp hypothermia could prevent CIA.
  · Prolonged-scalp hypothermia provides a higher degree of protection, whereas a short duration demonstrates a weaker protective effect.
  · However, this difference was not statistically significant.
