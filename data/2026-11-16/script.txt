◆ 배경 — Introduction
  · Apoptotic signaling also plays a critical role in AGA progression, particularly during the catagen phase.12 Increased TGF-β1 signaling in hair follicle epithelial compartments, including outer root sheath cells, promotes apoptosis and is associated with upregulation of pro-apoptotic factors such as Bcl-2-associated X protein (Bax) and cleaved caspase-3, along with downregulation of the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2.9, 13, 14 These molecular changes contribute to follicular miniaturization and irreversible hair loss.
  · Current pharmacological treatments for AGA include finasteride, a selective inhibitor of type 2 5α-reductase, and minoxidil, a vasodilator that promotes hair growth.15, 16, 17 Although finasteride effectively reduces DHT levels, its long-term use is associated with adverse effects, including sexual dysfunction, mood disturbances, and potential hepatotoxicity, raising concerns regarding systemic safety.18, 19 Consequently, there is growing interest in identifying safer, naturally derived alternatives—particularly orally administrable agents suitable for long-term management of AGA.
  · Connarus semidecandrus Jack, a medicinal plant belonging to the Connaraceae family, has been traditionally used in Southeast Asia for the treatment of parasitic infections, fever, allergic conditions, and gastrointestinal disorders.20, 21, 22, 23 Previous studies have reported its anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-apoptotic properties, as well as protective effects against ultraviolet B (UVB)-induced skin damage.24 Notably, topical application of C. semidecandrus ethanol extract (CE) has been shown to inhibit 5α-reductase activity and alleviate hair loss in experimental AGA models.25 However, despite these promising findings, the therapeutic potential of CE when administered orally—along with its systemic safety and bioactive constituents—remains largely unexplored.

◆ 연구 방법 — Methods
  · The entire plant material was air-dried at room temperature (20–22 °C), mechanically ground, and extracted with 70% ethanol for 24 h.
  · (T.P. 20 mg/kg/PBS/96% EtOH with propylene glycol), CE oral (T.P. 20 mg/kg/CE 300 mg/kg in PBS/96% EtOH with propylene glycol), CE dorsal (T.P. 20 mg/kg/PBS/CE 10 mg/kg in 96% EtOH with propylene glycol), finasteride oral (T.P. 20 mg/kg/finasteride 0.5 mg/kg in PBS/96% EtOH with propylene glycol), and finasteride dorsal (T.P. 20 mg/kg/PBS/finasteride 50 mg/kg in 96% EtOH with propylene glycol).
  · CE (300 mg/kg) or finasteride (0.5 mg/kg) dissolved in PBS was administered orally once daily, while CE (10 mg/kg) or finasteride (50 mg/kg) dissolved in 96% EtOH with propylene glycol was applied topically to the dorsal skin once daily for 18 days.

◆ 고찰 — Discussion
  · Our findings extend previous reports that Connarus semidecandrus possesses antioxidant and cytoprotective properties and that its ethanol extract alleviates hair loss when applied topically.25 In contrast to earlier studies focusing on topical application, the present study demonstrates that CE retains biological activity following oral administration, suggesting that systemic exposure can modulate hair follicle biology.
  · Previous studies have demonstrated that attenuation of follicular apoptosis contributes to the anti-alopecia effects of plant-derived agents.27, 28 Consistent with these findings, oral CE administration also mitigated apoptosis, as evidenced by increased Bcl-2 levels and decreased cleaved caspase-3 levels in dorsal skin.
  · Although miquelianin has been documented to possess anti-allergic and antiviral properties.30, 31 its potential role in hair loss has not been previously described.
  · Finally, the translation of these preclinical findings to human use remains challenging because herbal extracts require clinical standardization.16, 32, 33, 34 and interspecies differences in pharmacokinetics may influence therapeutic outcomes.35, 36, 37 Therefore, future research should characterize the pharmacokinetic profile and individual contributions of the bioactive constituents.
