◆ 배경 — Introduction
  · Hair shedding is a common concern in dermatology and a clinically relevant feature of androgenetic alopecia (AGA) and telogen effluvium (TE).1–3 Objective counts of shedding hairs can support differential assessment, longitudinal monitoring, treatment counselling, and evaluation of treatment response.4,5 Reliable measurement also matters because perceived hair loss is closely associated with anxiety, altered self-image, impaired coping, and reduced quality of life.6–11
  · Visual shedding scales are faster and easier to use, but they provide only subjective, semi-quantitative estimates.12,13 Variation between observers can complicate longitudinal comparison, communication with patients, and interpretation of apparent changes in shedding.
  · Smartphone images are already used in teledermatology, where image quality and clinical usefulness have been evaluated.14–16 However, although artificial intelligence performs well in some dermatological tasks, such as dermoscopy image classification,17,18 clinical use depends on generalizability and on whether the output addresses a defined need.19–21 Because shedding hairs are fine, curved, overlapping, and often tangled, automated counting still faces unique and unresolved challenges.

◆ 연구 방법 — Material and Methods
  · Images were partitioned by participant into training (18 participants, 3396 images), internal validation (6 participants, 1113 images), and independent testing (6 participants, 1121 images).
  · The model combined an EfficientNet-B0 encoder,22 a U-Net-style decoder,23 and a feature module based on strip pooling and deformable convolution.24,25 These operations were used to represent long, thin, curved, and tangled hair structures.
  · Independent-test performance was stratified by reference-count range (1–50, 51–100, 101–200, and >200 hairs).

◆ 고찰 — Discussion
  · Patients reporting increased hair loss tended to overestimate hair numbers, possibly reflecting the psychosocial burden of alopecia.6,8,10 Automated counting may therefore provide a more consistent reference for consultations in which perceived and measured shedding diverge.
  · Usable quantitative data were more frequent in the subsequent AI-assisted cohort than in the historical manual cohort (76.7% versus 29.2%), while RWT completion was 22.5 percentage points higher, although this difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.056).
  · The model MAE of 5.4 hairs was small relative to both population-level differences reported in previous RWT studies3,4 and the count ranges represented by existing semi-quantitative visual shedding scales,12,13 while remaining close in magnitude to the mean absolute disagreement of 3.8 hairs between independent manual counters.
  · Repeated time-separated collection sessions and paired adjudicated counts strengthened the reference standard, while the 56 naturally collected samples from 10 additional participants provided complementary evaluation outside the sequence-construction paradigm and broadened participant representation, thus validation remains limited in scale and diversity.

◆ 결론 — Conclusions
  · AI analysis of ordinary smartphone photographs quantified dark brown or black shedding hairs on white or light-colored backgrounds rapidly and with low error under the evaluated conditions.
  · It had lower error than human visual estimation and was associated with a higher proportion of usable quantitative RWT data in the subsequent AI-assisted cohort.
  · This photograph-based method has the potential to support objective hair-shedding assessment in clinical care and research, with further validation needed across broader populations and settings.
