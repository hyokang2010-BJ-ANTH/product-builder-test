[한눈에 보기]
- Background: Subpericranial hematoma (SPH) is unusual in adults and is usually seen in neonates as cephalohematoma.
- Closed scalp hematoma is usually not associated with infection and abscess formation.
- Case description: A 28-year-old male presented with complaints of swelling on the left forehead.

[상세 내용] (논문 전문 4개 섹션에서 발췌)

◆ 배경 — INTRODUCTION
  · Subgaleal hematoma (SGH) is commonly associated with traumatic head injury.
  · It may or may not be associated with intracranial injury.
  · This case report has been reported in line with the CARE criteria and revised SCARE criteria of 2023.

◆ 그 밖의 내용 — CASE PRESENTATION
  · A 28-year-old male presented with complaints of swelling on the left forehead anterior to the hairline for 2 months.
  · Postoperatively, the patient was kept on injection ceftriaxone 1 g bis in die (BD) and injection amikacin 500 mg OD for 3 days, followed by tablet faropenem 300 mg BD with tablet linezolid 600 mg BD for 14 days.

◆ 고찰 — DISCUSSION
  · The subpericranial space is a potential location between the overlying pericranium and skull bone below.
  · Pericranium is the deepest layer of scalp, which is adhered to the skull and harvested during neurosurgical procedures for duroplasty or defect coverage.
  · Hematoma collection in subpericranial location is commonly seen in newborns during the process of birth where they are termed cephalohematomas.
  · The exception mentioned was a 39-year-old female who had SGH contralateral to the scalp injury which was managed conservatively using compression bandage application.

◆ 결론 — CONCLUSION
  · Subpericranial abscess is an unreported complication of SPH in an adult.
  · SPH can get infected with procedures like microneedling without an open wound.
  · Deep scalp location makes this a surgical entity rather than an aspiration procedure.
