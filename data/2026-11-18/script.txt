제목: A Rare Post-Hair Transplant Complication With Complete Recovery
저널: Journal of medical cases
저자: Tcholadze E, Javakhadze M, Goliadze L 외
발행: 2026 Dec

[한눈에 보기]
- Postoperative complications following hair transplantation are generally mild and self-limited; however, severe infectious or inflammatory reactions involving the periorbital or cervicofacial regions are exceedingly rare yet potentially vision- or life-threatening.
- A 34-year-old man developed rapidly progressive cervicofacial edema 48 h after an uncomplicated hair transplantation procedure.
- Laboratory evaluation demonstrated markedly elevated inflammatory markers (C-reactive protein 193 mg/L (reference < 5 mg/L), procalcitonin 51.43 ng/mL (reference < 0.05 ng/mL), lactate 9.68 mmol/L (reference 0.5-2.2 mmol/L)) with progressive leukopenia.

[상세 내용] (논문 전문 3개 섹션에서 발췌)

◆ 배경 — Introduction
  · According to a recent large-scale review by Garg and Garg, postoperative complications—ranging from edema and folliculitis to rare cases of recipient-site necrosis—were identified in 2.8% of 2,896 patients undergoing hair transplantation procedures.
  · Androgenetic alopecia remains the leading indication for surgical hair restoration, affecting up to 80% of men and approximately 25% of women worldwide.
  · A 2022 review of postoperative outcomes described periorbital edema as one of the more frequent early complications, but noted that necrosis remains exceedingly rare, with only isolated cases reported in the literature.

◆ 그 밖의 내용 — Case Report
  · On admission, laboratory findings revealed markedly elevated inflammatory markers: C-reactive protein (CRP) 193 mg/L (reference range < 5 mg/L), procalcitonin (PCT) 51.43 ng/mL (reference range < 0.05 ng/mL), and lactate 9.68 mmol/L (reference range 0.5–2.2 mmol/L).
  · Leukopenia was also present (white blood cell (WBC) 3.41 × 109/L (reference range 4–10 × 109/L)).

◆ 고찰 — Discussion
  · Hair transplantation is widely regarded as a safe and minimally invasive surgical procedure with a low incidence of severe complications.
  · Most postoperative reactions are mild and transient, including edema, minor pain, pruritus, or folliculitis.
  · Serious complications such as extensive soft-tissue infection, necrosis, or systemic inflammatory response are exceedingly rare but have been described in isolated reports.
  · Postoperative facial edema is among the more common early reactions after hair transplantation.
