제목: Extracellular Vesicles for Androgenetic Alopecia: Current Evidence, Mechanisms, and Clinical Prospects

[한눈에 보기]
- While mainstream therapies for androgenetic alopecia (AGA), including pharmacological treatments, mesotherapy, low-level light therapy, and hair transplantation, often fall short of clinical needs due to limited efficacy, side effects, or invasiveness, regenerative medicine based on stem cells shows promise yet faces challenges related to immunogenicity, standardization, and safety.
- Against this backdrop, extracellular vesicles (EVs)-nanoscale signaling vesicles enclosed by a lipid bilayer-have rapidly emerged as an innovative "cell-free" therapeutic paradigm.
- By delivering bioactive "cargo" such as proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids, EVs mimic the therapeutic functions of their parent cells and precisely regulate recipient cells.

◆ 배경 — Introduction
  · Epidemiological data indicate its prevalence increases markedly with age, affecting up to 50% of women and 80% of men, and exerting a profound negative impact on patients’ psychosocial well-being and quality of life.1–3 The core pathophysiological mechanism involves an abnormal sensitivity of hair follicles to dihydrotestosterone (DHT), leading to excessive activation of androgen receptor signaling, dysfunction of dermal papilla cells, and disruption of the hair follicle cycle.
  · Regenerative medicine strategies based on tissue engineering and stem cell transplantation have brought new hope for AGA treatment, demonstrating the potential to reconstruct the hair follicle niche through cellular therapies.8,9 However, stem cell therapies face their own bottlenecks—such as immune rejection, tumorigenicity risks, survival uncertainty, and difficulties in standardized preparation—which hinder their clinical translation.10 Synthetic nanomedicine strategies, in addition to stem cell therapy, have also been explored as potential interventions for AGA, aiming to enhance the transdermal delivery efficiency and follicular targeting of drugs such as minoxidil through carrier systems like liposomes, solid lipid nanoparticles, nanostructured lipid carriers, and polymeric nanoparticles.11 However, the application of these synthetic nanocarrier systems in dermatology is limited by poor skin barrier penetration efficiency, questionable biocompatibility, and a lack of long-term safety data, resulting in slow clinical translation progress.12–14 These shared challenges in synthetic nanomedicine and regenerative medicine underscore the urgent need to develop novel nanoplatforms that combine inherent targeting capabilities with the potential for standardization.
  · They precisely regulate recipient cell functions, thereby mimicking the therapeutic effects of their parent cells while circumventing the risks associated with live-cell therapies.16,17 Compared to other emerging biological products such as platelet-rich plasma (PRP)—which is limited by compositional complexity, donor variability, and lack of standardized protocols, with multiple randomized controlled trials failing to replicate early positive findings18–20—and stem cell-conditioned media, whose active components are unstable, have short half-lives, and are prone to degradation,21 EVs, encapsulating their cargo within a lipid bilayer, exhibit superior stability and lower immunogenicity.22 Preliminary studies have confirmed the encouraging potential of EVs from various sources in promoting hair growth.23,24 A recent pilot study also showed that plasma- and sonophoresis-assisted topical delivery of exosomes improved hair density and diameter in 25 AGA patients with a favorable safety profile.25 The relevance of EVs to AGA has never been more timely: recent breakthroughs in scalable isolation, engineered cargo loading, and targeted delivery have substantially advanced their translational feasibility, while the persistent shortcomings of current pharmacological and cell-based therapies have created an urgent demand for safer, more standardized off-the-shelf alternatives.

◆ 그 밖의 내용 — Biological Characteristics and Preparation of EVs
  · Their ability to regulate recipient cell function by delivering bioactive “cargo” has garnered significant attention in the field of regenerative medicine.68,69 Based on their biogenesis pathways and physical size, EVs are primarily classified into three categories: exosomes (approximately 30–150 nm in diameter), which originate from the endocytic pathway and are released upon fusion of multivesicular bodies with the plasma membrane; microvesicles (approximately 100–1000 nm), which are generated by direct outward budding of the plasma membrane; and apoptotic bodies, produced during the process of apoptosis.70,71 The formation mechanism of exosomes is relatively well-defined, involving the inward budding of the limiting membrane to form intraluminal vesicles, the maturation of multivesicular bodies, and the process of exocytosis.
  · For example, applying mechanical stress (eg, fluid shear stress, stretching),86,87 altering culture conditions (eg, hypoxia, three-dimensional culture),88 or using biochemical stimuli (eg, cytokines, lipopolysaccharides)89,90 can significantly influence EV secretion rates and alter the abundance of their cargo (such as specific miRNAs or proteins), thereby directionally enhancing their efficacy in promoting angiogenesis, anti-inflammation, or tissue repair.91,92 This strategy of producing functionally enhanced, “customized” EVs by modulating cell states provides an important pathway for obtaining high-quality therapeutic EVs.

◆ 그 밖의 내용 — Plant-Derived Exosome-Like Nanoparticles
  · Ginger-derived exosome-like nanoparticles (GELNs) have recently emerged as a promising plant-based platform for AGA treatment, offering dual functionality as both active therapeutic agents and efficient drug delivery vehicles.55 GELNs, with sizes of 30–150 nm, possess intrinsic anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and bio-regulatory properties while exhibiting low immunogenicity and high delivery efficiency.186,187 To overcome the limitations of poor solubility and systemic adverse effects associated with oral finasteride—researchers developed a finasteride-loaded GELN system integrated into a thermosensitive poloxamer gel (FIN@GELNs-Gel) for enhanced transdermal delivery.55 The poloxamer-based gel exhibits sol‑gel transition at body temperature, facilitating drug loading at low temperature and sustained release upon topical application.188,189 In vitro, FIN@GELNs-Gel promoted cell migration and angiogenesis, suggesting improved perifollicular microenvironment remodeling.55 In an AGA mouse model, the formulation significantly reduced DHT levels in both skin and serum, upregulated VEGF and Ki67 expression, and effectively accelerated telogen‑to‑anagen transition.55 Importantly, FIN@GELNs-Gel demonstrated superior hair regeneration efficacy compared to oral finasteride, with reduced dosing frequency and minimized systemic toxicity risk.55 Although industrial scale‑up and drug loading capacity require further optimization, this integrated nanovesicle‑hydrogel platform offers a safe, effective, and patient‑friendly topical therapeutic strategy for AGA, with promising implications for improving treatment adherence and clinical translation.55

◆ 그 밖의 내용 — Milk-Derived EVs
  · The therapeutic value of mEVs is first reflected in their naturally carried bioactive “cargo”, with researchers demonstrating that exosomes isolated directly from bovine colostrum (Milk-exo) can effectively promote the proliferation of human DPCs and antagonize DHT-induced hair follicle growth arrest in vivo.56 In animal models, their efficacy in inducing hair growth was comparable to the first-line clinical drug minoxidil, with no significant adverse reactions reported.56 Mechanistically, Milk-exo drives telogen-to-anagen transition by activating the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, an effect attributed not to a single component (eg, lactoferrin) but to a “multifunctional active molecular complex” comprising over 1000 growth factors, cytokines, and miRNAs.198,199

◆ 그 밖의 내용 — Challenges in the Clinical Translation of EVs
  · Some studies exhibit shortcomings in the transparency of isolation methods, the exclusion of contaminants (eg, Calnexin), and multi-technique characterization, which weakens data reliability and cross-study comparability.23,206 This “fragmented” state is particularly pronounced in clinical research, manifesting as extreme inconsistency in key treatment parameters: the dose reporting of EVs (particle number vs protein mass) and the numerical range vary enormously (109 to 1011 particles/mL), and there is a lack of consensus on the route of administration (topical, injection, microneedling), frequency, and treatment duration (4 to 26 weeks).23,207 Furthermore, most studies are limited by small sample sizes, short follow-up periods (often ≤12 weeks), and homogeneous subject populations, severely restricting the evaluation of efficacy generalizability and long-term safety.29 A notable concern in the current literature is the lack of systematic reporting on neutral or negative outcomes, which may reflect publication bias rather than definitive evidence of efficacy.

◆ 결론 — Critical Comparison of EV Sources and Future Perspective
  · EVs, as an innovative “cell-free” therapeutic paradigm, represent a promising but still investigational approach in the treatment of AGA.
  · HEK-293/293T-derived engineered exosomes enable off-the-shelf production of rationally designed biologics; however, immunogenicity and long-term safety require rigorous investigation.
  · Ultimately, over the next 5–10 years, the convergence of these priorities—with regulatory clarity and high-quality clinical evidence at the core—will be essential for translating EV-based therapies into routine practice.

◆ 결론 — Conclusion
  · EVs represent a promising but still investigational cell-free therapeutic paradigm for AGA, capable of delivering complex bioactive cargo that synergistically activates Wnt/β-catenin, suppresses TGF-β/SMAD signaling, and restores the perifollicular microenvironment.
