- Purpose: Chemotherapy-induced alopecia (CIA) is a distressing side effect of cancer treatment that significantly impacts patients' quality of life.
- Methods: We conducted a mixed-methods, cross-sectional survey study of 101 adults who had received chemotherapy and experienced hair loss.
- Results: Thematic analysis revealed 11 key patient-identified themes, including emotional distress, provider influence, financial barriers, and a strong desire for autonomy in decision-making.

◆ 배경 — Introduction and background
  · Chemotherapy-induced hair loss, also referred to as chemotherapy-induced alopecia (CIA), is one of the most visible and emotionally distressing side effects of cancer treatment.
  · Prior research has documented associations between hair loss and reduced quality of life, body image concerns, and psychosocial distress, underscoring the clinical importance of addressing CIA as part of comprehensive cancer care.
  · Beyond physical appearance, hair loss can disrupt patients’ sense of privacy and social identity, serving as a visible marker of illness that persists throughout treatment and recovery.

◆ 연구 방법 — Methods
  · Eligible participants included adult individuals (at least 18 years old) who had received chemotherapy and experienced hair loss.
  · A total of 101 individuals participated in the survey study.
  · The survey was open from January to May 2025.

◆ 주요 결과 — Results
  · A total of 101 participants completed the survey.
  · Most participants had completed at least a bachelor’s degree (74.3%), and 56.4% were employed either full time or part time.
  · Among participants who reported household income (n = 99), 57.6% reported an annual household income of $100,000 or greater.
  · Open-ended survey responses revealed 11 distinct themes reflecting patients’ experiences with chemotherapy-induced hair loss, counseling, and access to supportive resources.

◆ 고찰 — Discussion
  · A survey study of oncologists and dermatologists (n = 103) found significant differences in how CIA was viewed.
  · Another study of oncologists (n = 155) found that 67.1% of providers felt that all patients should be offered scalp cooling therapy, but only 26.3% report regularly initiating conversations about scalp cooling therapy and only 36% felt that scalp cooling benefits were worth the effort and cost involved.
  · A survey study of women in Facebook groups created for scalp cooling therapy users (n = 219) found that key motivations for utilizing social media in scalp cooling therapy included to gain support from others going through a similar experience (88.1% of participants), to receive scalp cooling instructions (82.2%), and to learn about the scalp cooling therapy outcomes of other patients (69.9%).
  · In a study of 179 patients undergoing chemotherapy, 56.4% reported that hair loss was the worst side effect, with 11.7% finding it worse than the disease itself and 16.2% reporting that hair loss would have made them stop chemotherapy if it was not a life-saving treatment.

◆ 결론 — Conclusion
  · CIA remains a significant and often inadequately addressed aspect of cancer care.
  · Participants described gaps in counseling, variable access to preventive and management strategies, financial and logistical barriers, and unmet needs related to hair regrowth and psychosocial support.
  · Standardized, evidence-based CIA education incorporated into routine pre-chemotherapy counseling represents an important starting point for improving care by ensuring that patients receive consistent information about anticipated hair loss and available prevention and management options.
