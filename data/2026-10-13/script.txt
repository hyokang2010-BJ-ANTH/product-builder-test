발행: 2026
원문: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42857222/

[한눈에 보기]
- Postpartum alopecia (PPA) is a common physiological hair loss in women, characterized by complex pathogenesis involving abrupt postpartum hormonal withdrawal, disrupted hair follicle cycling, local microenvironmental inflammation, and oxidative stress.
- Umbilical cord blood-derived extracellular vesicles (UCB-EVs)-nanosized, lipid-bilayer-enclosed particles enriched with microRNAs, growth factors, and proteins-have shown considerable promise in regenerative medicine owing to their low immunogenicity, high biocompatibility, and tissue-repair capacity.
- This review systematically summarizes the current state of research on UCB-EVs in the context of PPA, focusing on their proposed molecular mechanisms in hair follicle regeneration, hair-cycle modulation, antiinflammatory effects, and antioxidative stress responses.

◆ 배경 — Introduction
  · Female postpartum alopecia (PPA) represents a significant clinical and psychosocial challenge, emerging as one of the most common dermatological conditions affecting women in the postpartum period.
  · Epidemiological data indicates that the prevalence of PPA is remarkably high, with studies suggesting that over 90% of postpartum women experience some degree of hair loss (3).
  · At the molecular level, UCB-EVs are enriched with specific miRNAs, such as miR-21-5p and let-7b-5p, which are capable of targeting and modulating key signaling pathways critical for hair follicle biology (2).
