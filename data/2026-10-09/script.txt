◆ 배경 — Background
  · Hair transplantation is a widely performed office-based aesthetic procedure characterized by prolonged procedural duration, cumulative local anesthetic exposure, and reliance on sustained patient cooperation.
  · Despite extensive literature on surgical technique and aesthetic outcomes, periprocedural anesthetic and analgesic considerations remain comparatively undersynthesized.

◆ 배경 — Objective
  · The aims of this study were to review periprocedural anesthesia and analgesia strategies in hair transplantation and highlight practical considerations related to patient safety, procedural workflow, and intraoperative management.

◆ 연구 방법 — Methods
  · A structured literature review was conducted using PubMed/MEDLINE and Embase from database inception through January 2026.
  · Studies evaluating anesthetic techniques, analgesic strategies, periprocedural pain management, and anesthesia-related safety considerations in hair transplantation and comparable office-based dermatologic surgical procedures were included.
  · Evidence was synthesized qualitatively according to predefined clinical domains.

◆ 주요 결과 — Results
  · Available evidence supports multimodal anesthetic strategies incorporating individualized local anesthetic selection, tumescence, regional nerve blocks, adjunctive analgesia, and structured intraoperative monitoring to optimize patient comfort, procedural efficiency, and safety.
  · However, available evidence consists predominantly of observational studies, case series, and expert consensus, with limited high-quality comparative data.

◆ 결론 — Conclusion
  · Individualized multimodal anesthetic strategies represent the current evidence-informed approach to periprocedural management in hair transplantation.
  · Prospective comparative studies are needed to strengthen the evidence base and inform future clinical recommendations.
