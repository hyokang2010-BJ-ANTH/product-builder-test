[상세 내용] (초록 4개 항목 정리 — 이 논문은 전문이 아직 공개되지 않아 초록을 씁니다)

◆ 배경 — Background
  · Mast cells are key regulators of cutaneous inflammation and are known to accumulate in response to various immunological stimuli.
  · Although their role in several dermatological conditions has been established, their specific contribution to different forms of alopecia-particularly scarring versus nonscarring types-remains incompletely understood.
  · This study aimed to compare the degree of mast cell infiltration across three distinct types of alopecia: androgenetic alopecia (AGA), fibrosing alopecia in a pattern distribution (FAPD), and lichen planopilaris (LPP).

◆ 연구 방법 — Method
  · A total of 56 patients diagnosed with AGA, FAPD, or LPP were enrolled.
  · Scalp biopsies were obtained from all participants, and diagnoses were confirmed through histopathological examination.
  · Mast cell distribution and infiltration density were quantified and analyzed using SPSS and Stata software.
  · The study was conducted with full ethical approval.

◆ 주요 결과 — Result
  · The majority of participants (91%) were female, with a mean age of 44.3 years.
  · FAPD was the most prevalent diagnosis (42.9%).
  · LPP demonstrated the highest degree of mast cell infiltration, while AGA exhibited the lowest (ANOVA, p < 0.001).
  · Post hoc analysis revealed significant differences between AGA and FAPD (-17.7%, p = 0.001) and between AGA and LPP (-20.1%, p = 0.001).
  · Although mast cell infiltration was higher in patients over 40 years of age, this difference did not reach statistical significance.
  · No significant association was identified between mast cell infiltration and patient sex.

◆ 결론 — Conclusion
  · Mast cell infiltration is significantly more pronounced in scarring alopecias (LPP and FAPD) compared to non-scarring AGA, suggesting a shared immunopathological mechanism underlying follicular destruction in scarring subtypes.
  · These findings also raise important questions regarding the potential inflammatory contribution of mast cells to AGA pathogenesis.
